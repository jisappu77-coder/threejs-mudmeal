The source axes are east/north/up. The mapping to Three.js is `[x + originX, z + originZ, -y - originY]`. Zone origins are applied once. Road endpoints extending beyond a block are clipped at its boundary; paired scenes continue the same road. The four-metre to six-metre taper at the heritage/market seam is interpolated from the supplied profile. Both zones overlap in their local reference coordinates and are kept separate; an invented inter-phase road would violate the supplied placement contract.

Geometry is derived from the JSON rather than an image used as a skybox or ground plane. Camera presets change the camera only. The master/player captures produced by the browser check contain actual modeled WebGL geometry. Reference overlays are explicitly labeled. Runtime audits compare modeled building footprint records with the calibrated layouts; paired endpoint positions and widths are checked directly.

V2 changes Phase 1 to an elevated oblique miniature-city view. Its build guide prioritizes unchanged metre-scale roads, footprints and seams; camera positions are approximate starting points. The Phase 1 runtime therefore no longer moves or compresses the cafe, bakery, pier, shore, buildings or connections, and no longer bends the waterfront. The 8-metre waterfront road, full promenade and supplied entrances are retained. An elevated follow camera (44 degrees for the waterfront, 48 degrees for the heritage side view, and 40 degrees elsewhere) keeps roofs, traffic and water visible while the rider stays at its original physical scale. Other reference presets use the revised archive cameras. Phase 2 retains its urban calibration.

Scene 1 includes two cafe storefront faces, warm framed glazing, shutter window planters, the teal bakery display, potted promenade planting, drooping palm crowns with individual folded leaflets, shoreline boulders and water plants. Traffic, chairs and people are positioned around the restored source building lots. The pier retains its specified footprint and single-pitch canopy. The independent images can disagree about canopy materials and facade details; source geometry is authoritative. Windows, foliage and material maps remain authored approximations.

The user requested identical appearance. That acceptance target has **not** been met or certified by this implementation. Exact pixel agreement would require resolving conflicting views and additional asset/material and camera calibration work. Automated checks establish functional and spatial properties, not visual identity.

Gameplay is a small delivery loop with collection, customer delivery, countdown and rewards. Rivals, combat, interiors, dismounting/pier walking and weather variants are not implemented. Vehicles are fixed scene props with box collision rather than a general traffic physics engine. Delivery bays on the mainland keep pier deliveries off the pier. Buildings, walls and water constrain the rider. Vehicle props remain visible in all camera modes; pedestrian motion is stopped in reference camera modes. Open edges outside the specified zone are blocked by the playable bounds.

Rendering defaults to 30 FPS, caps pixel density at 2 across the renderer and color composer, uses a 2048-pixel filtered directional shadow map with an explicitly updated street-sized shadow projection, pauses simulation/rendering in hidden tabs, skips redraws of static reference cameras, uses GPU fences to avoid queued frames and reports a view ready only after its GPU fence completes, and merges fixed geometry by material, shadow flag and thirty-metre spatial cells so distant geometry can be culled. Shadow maps refresh when a scene/camera changes, the light region moves by a metre, or every eighth rendered frame; static scenery does not need a full shadow redraw on every frame. Northeast directional sunlight, cool sky fill, warm reflected ground light and ACES tone mapping restore the reference’s stronger sun/shade contrast. Alpha-aware ambient occlusion runs on small and large viewports. Its buffer is capped at 75% of CSS resolution and 768 pixels on the longest side, independently of the full-resolution color pass. Bloom is omitted. Banana leaves and tree branches use curved alpha-cut surfaces. Phase 1 palms use individual folded leaflets, a central spine and layered drooping fronds; shrubs have folded modeled leaves. A warm limewash map supplies wall color and bump detail; smoother asphalt adds diffuse grain; roofs use instanced barrel tiles and storefronts use framed glazing, lamps and shelving. Palm trunks taper with irregular bark rings. Small lamp halos are excluded from the ambient-occlusion depth pass to avoid solid rectangle artifacts. Pavement uses a larger, low-contrast tile atlas. A planted, modeled headland with cottages extends beyond the northern playable waterfront bounds; low-rise neighborhood continuation stays outside the four specified playable blocks. Generated café, bakery, restaurant and grocery interior textures sit behind the modeled windows, and a generated cumulus sky provides environment lighting. These are individual material assets rather than a reference image pasted behind the game. The sea shader uses world-space wave normals, sky reflections and sunlight highlights. The reference gallery preserves each supplied image’s aspect ratio and exports actual browser screenshots using `scripts/export-captures.mjs` after a successful sixteen-view check. The game autorickshaw uses round front lamps, a dark nose and a seated driver while the model review retains the original front styling. Fixed vehicle parts are merged while the movable bike retains its parent transform and the rider retains its skinned rig. Physical phone heat and sustained device frame rates have not been measured.

The latest player-view refinement uses warmer cream wall colors and corrected outward roof-tile and vehicle-panel normals. The hatchback has a taller, body-colored cabin, vertical rear lamps and a yellow registration plate; car and auto collision bounds include their instance scale. Café storefronts include modeled glazing and lanterns, and menu boards use tilted wooden A-frames with readable chalk panels. ## Graphics refinement after V2 publication

The follow-up pass restores contact shading below 900 CSS pixels and native high-density color rendering up to 2×. Phase 1 gains larger clay barrel tiles with irregular terracotta colors, lightly beveled building edges, layered cornices, window lintels and brackets, fuller flower boxes, segmented raised curbs and narrow gutters. The waterfront cafe and home gardens gain layered planting without changing the source road, bay or building bounds. Palm leaflets cast real silhouette shadows. Grass has multiscale surface variation, and the sea uses irregular wave perturbations and color patches instead of regular sine stripes. Exposure is reduced from 1.25 to 1.15 to retain material color in direct sunlight.

Visual review uses actual Scene 1 browser captures, followed by all eight Phase 1 master/player captures, source-dimension and connected-route checks. These improve the authored scene; they do not certify an exact match to the supplied illustration. High-density rendering and always-on occlusion still require real-device performance evaluation.
