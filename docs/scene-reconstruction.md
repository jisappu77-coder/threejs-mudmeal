SHA-256 comparison confirmed that all 54 extracted files match the previously reassembled Phase 1 content; only the archive root name changed. `docs/phase1-source.json` records the archive and per-file hashes. Phase 2 was extracted from `Mud_Meals_Phase_2_Kochi_Urban_All_Scenes.zip`. ZIP integrity was checked while extracting. The original archives remain outside the repository under `/workspace/references`.

The source axes are east/north/up. The mapping to Three.js is `[x + originX, z + originZ, -y - originY]`. Zone origins are applied once. Road endpoints extending beyond a block are clipped at its boundary; paired scenes continue the same road. The four-metre to six-metre taper at the heritage/market seam is interpolated from the supplied profile. Both zones overlap in their local reference coordinates and are kept separate; an invented inter-phase road would violate the supplied placement contract.

Geometry is derived from the JSON rather than an image used as a skybox or ground plane. Camera presets change the camera only. The master/player captures produced by the browser check contain actual modeled WebGL geometry. Reference overlays are explicitly labeled. Runtime audits compare modeled building footprint records with the calibrated layouts; paired endpoint positions and widths are checked directly.

The original archives explicitly state that images are generated reference illustrations, not calibrated views of one common mesh. They do not certify exact multi-view consistency. Architectural detail, foliage density and projected dimensions vary among images. The reconstruction starts with supplied footprints, entrances, heights, roof categories and major props. Fixed runtime calibration in `src/layout.js` moves the waterfront café, bakery, west junction, pier, parked vehicles and palms and sets a shared 6.2-metre waterfront road and narrows the shore. The fishing-net block has a narrower and taller provision shop, a steeper seafood roof, shifted net platforms/pivots and corresponding railing gaps. Heritage and market shop fronts move toward the curb together with entrances, compounds and pickup bays. The heritage home roof and chapel tower are taller, with additional palms in the yards to reproduce the player view’s skyline. Urban north/south player streets use a 4.5-metre carriageway, with narrowed bridge clearance and matching connection widths, and have smaller setbacks; the restaurant-junction foreground lots and background bakery/apartment are shortened and moved closer to the image junction. Its cross street bends to retain the original boundary connections. The commercial block has a closer bus stop and shorter foreground shops; the canal block moves its banks and bridge together, with verified water clearance and open bridge rails. Loading-lane lots have smaller setbacks and an open warehouse door. `src/projection.js` bends the distant waterfront consistently across scenery and actors. Calibrated road/path seams and water collision remain checked. Player cameras are image-calibrated chase views, including a lower heritage chase camera and a revised fishing-net viewing direction; other camera coordinates come from the source specifications. Windows, foliage and material maps are authored approximations. Signage uses readable English business labels; reliable reviewed Malayalam text was not supplied. No unreviewed generated glyphs are treated as readable Malayalam.

The user requested identical appearance. That acceptance target has **not** been met or certified by this implementation. Exact pixel agreement would require resolving conflicting views and additional asset/material and camera calibration work. Automated checks establish functional and spatial properties, not visual identity.

Gameplay is a small delivery loop with collection, customer delivery, countdown and rewards. Rivals, combat, interiors, dismounting/pier walking and weather variants are not implemented. Vehicles are fixed scene props with box collision rather than a general traffic physics engine. Delivery bays on the mainland keep pier deliveries off the pier. Buildings, walls and water constrain the rider. Vehicle props remain visible in all camera modes; pedestrian motion is stopped in reference camera modes. Open edges outside the specified zone are blocked by the playable bounds.

Rendering defaults to 30 FPS, caps pixel density at 1.5, uses a 2048-pixel filtered directional shadow map with an explicitly updated street-sized shadow projection, pauses simulation/rendering in hidden tabs, skips redraws of static reference cameras, uses GPU fences to avoid queued frames and reports a view ready only after its GPU fence completes, and merges fixed geometry by material, shadow flag and thirty-metre spatial cells so distant geometry can be culled. Shadow maps refresh when a scene/camera changes, the light region moves by a metre, or every eighth rendered frame; static scenery does not need a full shadow redraw on every frame. Northeast directional sunlight, cool sky fill, warm reflected ground light and ACES tone mapping restore the reference’s stronger sun/shade contrast. Desktop rendering adds alpha-aware ambient occlusion; bloom is omitted to avoid the roof glare seen during earlier comparisons. Small viewports omit ambient occlusion. Transparent generated foliage textures are curved onto modeled leaves, subtle procedural plaster supplies the wall color while a weathered map supplies bump detail; asphalt adds diffuse grain; roofs use instanced barrel tiles and storefronts use framed glazing, lamps and shelving. Organic coconut-frond cutouts replace the comb-shaped palm texture, and trunks taper with irregular bark rings. Pavement uses a larger, low-contrast tile atlas. A planted, modeled headland with cottages extends beyond the northern playable waterfront bounds; background cottages inside vacant lots have collision and avoid roads and primary buildings. Generated café, bakery, restaurant and grocery interior textures sit behind the modeled windows, and a generated sky provides the environment lighting. These are individual material assets rather than a reference image pasted behind the game. The sea shader uses world-space wave normals, sky reflections and sunlight highlights. The reference gallery preserves each supplied image’s aspect ratio and exports actual browser screenshots using `scripts/export-captures.mjs` after a successful sixteen-view check. The game autorickshaw uses round front lamps, a dark nose and a seated driver while the model review retains the original front styling. Fixed vehicle parts are merged while the movable bike retains its parent transform and the rider retains its skinned rig. Physical phone heat and sustained device frame rates have not been measured.

The latest player-view refinement uses warmer cream wall colors and corrected outward roof-tile and vehicle-panel normals. The hatchback has a taller, body-colored cabin, vertical rear lamps and a yellow registration plate; car and auto collision bounds include their instance scale. Café entrances add a modeled arch and lanterns, and menu boards use tilted wooden A-frames with readable chalk panels. Added background cottages are checked against road and primary-building clearances. These changes improve visible details but do not establish an identical match to the supplied illustrations.

Palm crowns use a smoother cutout texture with broad leaflets, narrower, shorter fronds with a shallower tip droop. The delivery rider has a wider body and slightly flatter helmet; inverse-kinematics contact targets compensate for the rider width so hands and feet retain their bike contact positions.
